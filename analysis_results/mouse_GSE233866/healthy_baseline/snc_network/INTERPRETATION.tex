The soft-thresholding power scan selects $\beta=5$ as the lowest power clearing
the scale-free fit threshold ($R^2 \geq 0.8$; $R^2=0.801$ at $\beta=5$, versus
$0.684$ at $\beta=4$), giving a well-behaved, non-arbitrary basis for the
network. Clustering on 2{,}226 pooled SNc cells (6 animals) yields 9 modules,
a modest number consistent with a relatively homogeneous cell population.

CACNA1D is assigned to the \textbf{turquoise} module with $\mathrm{kME}=0.048$ ---
numerically very weak, placing the gene near the low-connectivity tail of its
module rather than among its hub genes. Taken alone this would suggest CACNA1D
is only loosely embedded in SNc's co-expression structure.

That reading is complicated, and partly justified, by the enrichment result:
turquoise is significantly enriched for the KEGG term \emph{Dopaminergic synapse}
($p_{\mathrm{adj}} = 2.8\times10^{-4}$), directly on-topic for a dopaminergic
neuron dataset. So while CACNA1D itself is not a central gene, the module it
belongs to is functionally coherent and biologically meaningful for this cell
type --- the weak kME reflects CACNA1D's own position within a real program,
not noise in the program itself.
