\subsection*{Network construction}

VTA's soft power scan independently selects $\beta=5$ ($R^2=0.850$), matching
SNc's choice by coincidence of the data rather than by assumption --- each
network's power was fit separately from its own \texttt{soft\_power\_table.csv}.
Clustering on only 832 pooled VTA cells (versus SNc's 2{,}226) yields 39
modules, more than four times SNc's count. This is best read as a
sample-size artifact rather than a genuine difference in how organized
VTA's co-expression structure is: fewer cells produce noisier gene-gene
correlations, which tends to fragment clustering into many small modules
rather than a few large, stable ones. Many of VTA's 39 modules are under
100 genes, several under 20.

\subsection*{CACNA1D's position}

CACNA1D is assigned to the \textbf{brown} module (316 genes) with
$\mathrm{kME}=0.296$ --- weak-to-moderate, and \emph{lower} than its SNc
value (0.442), the opposite of what this document previously (incorrectly)
reported. Brown's top hub genes reach kME 0.62--0.75:
\texttt{Aldh1a1} (0.751), \texttt{Kcnab1} (0.744), \texttt{Pde1c} (0.711),
\texttt{Tmem132d} (0.681), \texttt{Dmd} (0.671), \texttt{Cdh8} (0.657),
\texttt{Ehbp1} (0.633), \texttt{Ebf1} (0.624) --- none of which overlap
with SNc turquoise's top hub genes, a first hint that VTA's CACNA1D
neighborhood is a distinct gene community, not just a smaller sample of
the same one. \emph{(Correction, 2026-08-13: this document previously
reported $\mathrm{kME}=0.094$ and described it as roughly double the SNc
value; that was misread from the wrong \texttt{kME\_*} column in
\texttt{modules.csv}. 0.296 is \texttt{kME\_brown} for Cacna1d,
re-verified directly against the source file --- SNc, not VTA, has the
stronger connection.)}

\subsection*{Enrichment}

Brown is significantly enriched for six terms (all $p_{\mathrm{adj}} <
0.003$): \emph{Neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction}
($p_{\mathrm{adj}}=1.37\times10^{-4}$, \texttt{KEGG\_2019\_Mouse}),
\emph{Anterograde trans-synaptic signaling} and \emph{Chemical synaptic
transmission} (both $p_{\mathrm{adj}}=3.64\times10^{-4}$, GO),
\emph{Sensory perception of mechanical stimulus}
($p_{\mathrm{adj}}=1.21\times10^{-3}$, GO), \emph{Axon guidance}
($p_{\mathrm{adj}}=1.61\times10^{-3}$, GO), and \emph{Calcium signaling
pathway} ($p_{\mathrm{adj}}=2.34\times10^{-3}$, \texttt{KEGG\_2019\_Mouse})
--- directly on-topic for a calcium-channel gene in a synaptic module,
though these enrichment values are individually weaker than SNc turquoise's
strongest hit and the module itself is functionally broader (general
synaptic transmission rather than a dopamine-specific pathway).

\subsection*{Not reproducible across cohorts, unlike SNc}

Where SNc turquoise's gene membership recurs strongly across two
independently-built networks from a separate animal cohort (75\% and 47\%
overlap; see \texttt{../snc\_network/INTERPRETATION.tex}), VTA brown does
not: of its 316 genes, only 18 (5.7\%) reappear in
\texttt{../../lesion\_model/vta\_lesioned\_network/}'s blue module (which
itself contains 695 genes, so this is 2.6\% overlap from that side too).
None of brown's top eight hub genes recur as hubs there. Combined with
VTA's fragmented 39-module structure above, this is consistent with VTA's
networks here being considerably less stable estimates than SNc's ---
plausibly a direct consequence of VTA's much smaller cell counts (832 vs.
2{,}226 at baseline; 1{,}720 vs.\ 2{,}291 in the lesion arm) rather than a
claim that VTA's true biology is less organized. Read VTA-specific module
claims in this project with more caution than the equivalent SNc claims.

\subsection*{Regional comparison}

Both regions place CACNA1D in a functionally sensible, moderately-central
synaptic module --- SNc's connection is the stronger of the two, and its
module's enrichment is both stronger and more dopamine-specific. The formal
statistical test of whether SNc's neighborhood specifically (not VTA's)
holds up across regions is \texttt{../snc\_vs\_vta\_preservation/}, which
uses SNc as the reference precisely because it is the larger, more stable
network of the pair.
