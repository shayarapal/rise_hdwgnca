\subsection*{Network construction}

The intact-hemisphere SNc network, built from 8{,}637 cells (the largest
single-condition population in this project), independently selects a soft
power of 6 ($R^2 = 0.959$) --- higher than the healthy-baseline SNc network's
power of 5, consistent with this being a different animal cohort under a
different experimental arm rather than a repeat of the same data. Clustering
resolves 1{,}640 genes into CACNA1D's module alone, the largest
CACNA1D-containing module of any network in this project.

\subsection*{CACNA1D's position}

CACNA1D falls in the \textbf{blue} module here with $\mathrm{kME}=0.346$,
below blue's top hub genes: \texttt{Klhl1} (0.826), \texttt{Robo2} (0.817),
\texttt{Rgs6} (0.807), \texttt{Pbx1} (0.802), \texttt{Kcnj6} (0.801),
\texttt{9530059O14Rik} (0.798), \texttt{Nos1ap} (0.797), \texttt{Frmd4b}
(0.793). Four of these eight --- \texttt{Klhl1}, \texttt{Pbx1},
\texttt{Rgs6}, \texttt{Nos1ap} --- are also top-eight hub genes of SNc
turquoise in the completely separate healthy-baseline cohort
(\texttt{../../healthy\_baseline/snc\_network/}), and the two modules share
1{,}009 genes overall (75\% of turquoise's 1{,}352). This is strong,
independent (non-statistical) evidence that SNc carries one consistent,
reproducible CACNA1D-adjacent gene community across at least two unrelated
animal cohorts, regardless of which color label a given clustering run
happens to assign it.
\emph{(Correction, 2026-08-13: this document previously compared against
0.048/0.094 for the two healthy-baseline networks and called 0.346
``substantially stronger than either'' --- both those figures were misread
from the wrong \texttt{kME\_*} column; the corrected healthy-baseline
values are 0.442 (SNc) and 0.296 (VTA).)}

\subsection*{Enrichment}

Blue is enriched for a tightly axon/synapse-themed set of GO terms, more
sharply focused than SNc turquoise's mixed synaptic/circadian/addiction
profile: \emph{Axon guidance} ($p_{\mathrm{adj}}=1.58\times10^{-7}$),
\emph{Modulation of chemical synaptic transmission} and \emph{Neuron
projection guidance} (both $p_{\mathrm{adj}}=4.88\times10^{-5}$),
\emph{Axonogenesis} ($p_{\mathrm{adj}}=7.86\times10^{-5}$), \emph{Regulation
of cell migration} ($p_{\mathrm{adj}}=2.56\times10^{-4}$), and
\emph{Cell-cell adhesion via plasma-membrane adhesion molecules}
($p_{\mathrm{adj}}=2.93\times10^{-4}$). The much lower $p$-values here
relative to the healthy-baseline network reflect this network's larger cell
count (8{,}637 vs.\ 2{,}226), not necessarily a stronger biological signal.

\subsection*{Not directly comparable to the healthy-baseline kME}

\textbf{This network's kME is not directly comparable to the
healthy-baseline networks' kME, and should not be read as a before/after
value.} The healthy-baseline arm (6 untreated animals) and this lesion arm
(a different 6 animals) are separate cohorts, each independently clustered
into its own network with its own module composition --- a numeric
difference between them reflects cohort/batch variation as much as
biology. The comparison this network \emph{is} built for is the paired one
within this same lesion-arm cohort: intact (this network, 0.346) versus
lesioned (\texttt{../snc\_lesioned\_network/}, 0.398), same six animals,
contralateral hemispheres each. That comparison is the basis for the
"connectivity increases after lesioning" claim, and the formal version of
it is the DME test in \texttt{../lesioned\_vs\_intact\_combined\_dme\_snc/}.
This network's main use on its own is as a sanity check that CACNA1D's
connectivity in intact tissue is not itself an outlier before interpreting
how it changes after lesioning, and as one of the two networks whose raw
gene-set overlap with each other (792 of 1{,}640 blue genes, 48\%, reappear
in \texttt{../snc\_lesioned\_network/}'s smaller 855-gene brown module)
provides a non-statistical cross-check on the formal DME result.
