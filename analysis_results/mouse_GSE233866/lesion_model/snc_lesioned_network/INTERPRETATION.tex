\subsection*{Network construction}

6-OHDA lesioning collapses the SNc population from 8{,}637 to 2{,}291 cells
(3.8$\times$ loss), and the network built from what remains needs a higher
soft power (7, $R^2=0.905$) than the intact-hemisphere network (6) to reach
the same scale-free fit quality --- expected behavior when a smaller,
survivor-biased cell population produces a noisier correlation structure
that needs a stronger threshold to look properly scale-free.

\subsection*{CACNA1D's position}

CACNA1D moves to the \textbf{brown} module (855 genes) with
$\mathrm{kME}=0.398$, up from 0.346 in the intact network. Brown's top hub
genes reach kME 0.73--0.78: \texttt{Slc35f1} (0.783), \texttt{Sgcz}
(0.774), \texttt{Pbx1} (0.759), \texttt{Pex5l} (0.759), \texttt{Kcnj6}
(0.752), \texttt{Zfhx3} (0.737), \texttt{Galntl6} (0.736), \texttt{Nos1ap}
(0.728) --- five of which (\texttt{Slc35f1}, \texttt{Pbx1}, \texttt{Kcnj6},
\texttt{Zfhx3}, \texttt{Nos1ap}) were also hub-adjacent in the intact
network's blue module or healthy-baseline turquoise, underscoring that
lesioning reshuffles which genes are most central within a persisting
neighborhood rather than replacing the neighborhood outright: 792 of this
module's 855 genes (93\%) were already present in the intact network's
larger 1{,}640-gene blue module.

Read qualitatively, the kME increase points the same direction as the
human PD comparison elsewhere in this project: CACNA1D becomes \emph{more}
central to its co-expression module after disease/lesioning, not less.
This standalone comparison is suggestive but not a statistical test in
itself --- the two networks were clustered independently, so module
identity and kME are not directly comparable at the significance level.
The formal version of this claim is the DME test in
\texttt{../lesioned\_vs\_intact\_combined\_dme\_snc/}, which pools both
conditions into one shared network before testing, and which does confirm
a highly significant shift ($p_{\mathrm{adj}}=6.1\times10^{-269}$) --- in
the eigengene's overall level, not the kME reported here; the two
statistics answer related but distinct questions (see that document for
the distinction).

\subsection*{Enrichment}

Brown remains enriched for the same broad synaptic/axon-guidance theme as
the intact network, though with markedly weaker $p$-values, consistent with
its much smaller cell count (2{,}291 vs.\ 8{,}637): \emph{Modulation of
chemical synaptic transmission} ($p_{\mathrm{adj}}=1.27\times10^{-4}$),
\emph{Cell-cell adhesion via plasma-membrane adhesion molecules}
($p_{\mathrm{adj}}=8.12\times10^{-4}$), \emph{Axon guidance} and
\emph{Nervous system development} (both $p_{\mathrm{adj}}=1.67\times10^{-3}$),
\emph{Homophilic cell adhesion via plasma membrane adhesion molecules}
($p_{\mathrm{adj}}=1.67\times10^{-3}$), and \emph{Neuron projection
guidance} ($p_{\mathrm{adj}}=4.18\times10^{-3}$). The functional theme is
stable across lesioning; what changes is CACNA1D's position within it and
the eigengene's overall level (per the DME test), not what kind of program
this module represents.

\subsection*{Hub genes more central than CACNA1D}

of 855 genes. 206 genes outrank it. The nine below were curated from that
pool for plausible mechanistic relevance to SNc pacemaking, calcium-channel
biology, or Parkinson's disease --- not by raw kME alone --- and checked
against the published literature (independent of this project's own data)
for a documented CACNA1D/Ca$_v$1.3-specific connection. This is the same
gene set assessed in \texttt{../snc\_intact\_network/} and
\texttt{../lesioned\_vs\_intact\_combined\_dme\_snc/}, so ranks/kME can be
compared directly across disease state.

\paragraph{Rgs6 drops out of this module under lesioning.} In both other
networks, \texttt{Rgs6} is CACNA1D's single most relevant hub gene (Strong
tier, rank 3--17). Here it is \emph{not} a brown-module member at all --- it
is assigned to the separate pink module, even though its correlation to
brown remains high (kME$_{\mathrm{brown}}$=0.591, still above CACNA1D's own
0.398). This is a genuine hdWGCNA module-reassignment, not missing data:
after lesioning, Rgs6's expression pattern best matches a different gene
community than CACNA1D's, worth noting as a lesion-induced structural change
rather than treating it as absent from the dataset.

\paragraph{Two cautions.} \texttt{Klhl1} is this module's rank-17 gene
(kME=0.704) but is deliberately excluded from the table above: published
electrophysiology shows it modulates Ca$_v$2.1 (P/Q-type) and Ca$_v$3.2
(T-type) currents and specifically does \emph{not} affect Ca$_v$1.2/Ca$_v$1.3
--- evidence against a CACNA1D link despite its high rank.
\texttt{Adarb2} (rank 12) is a false friend: the documented CACNA1D
IQ-domain RNA-editing story belongs to a \emph{different} gene, \texttt{Adarb1}
(ADAR2) --- Adarb2 encodes ADAR3, a catalytically inactive paralog that
inhibits the active ADARs rather than editing anything itself.

\paragraph{Everything else above CACNA1D.} Of the remaining $\sim$196 genes
ranked above CACNA1D in this module, none surfaced a documented
CACNA1D/Ca$_v$1.3-specific literature connection during search --- including
\texttt{Nos1ap} (a well-known L-type-channel modulator, but for CACNA1C in
cardiac tissue, not CACNA1D in SNc), \texttt{Erc2} (a VGCC-clustering active-zone
protein, but for Ca$_v$2.1, not Ca$_v$1.3), and \texttt{Slc6a3} (DAT, a
canonical co-expressed SNc marker with no documented direct DAT--Ca$_v$1.3
interaction). Co-expression in this dataset does not by itself imply a
functional interaction; these are reported as co-expression-only findings.
